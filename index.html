The connection with Seafertil

My thesis: extracting value from biological resources

University of Valencia, Spain · January 2026

Obtaining and characterising antioxidant extracts from plant by-products and Spirulina

I worked on the extraction of bioactive compounds from different agri-food materials: Spirulina, sweet potato peel and orange peel. What interested me most was how green extraction technologies could help recover valuable compounds from these materials.

I worked with two technologies, Pulsed Electric Fields (PEF) and Pressurized Liquid Extraction (PLE or ASE), to obtain extracts rich in bioactive compounds and then characterise their properties.

In the case of Spirulina, we freeze-dried the extract and incorporated it into a beef burger formulation. We studied its potential as a natural colourant and antioxidant, particularly in relation to lipid oxidation and product shelf life.

Note: My thesis application was in food, not agriculture. I have not developed agricultural biostimulants before.

  1. SourceSpirulina and plant by-productsSweet potato peel and orange peel too.
  2. Extraction 1PLE/ASEPressurized liquid extraction, with water as solvent.
  3. Extraction 2PEFPulsed electric fields, also with water.
  4. AnalysisAntioxidants and proteinTotal polyphenols, ABTS and ORAC assays, protein.
  5. ApplicationBeef burgersFive days in refrigeration. pH and colour checked.

What I found

  • Spirulina was the source with the highest bioactive content, in antioxidant capacity and in protein.
  • PLE/ASE gave higher extraction yields in general. PEF was good at keeping heat-sensitive compounds.
  • Burgers with Spirulina extract, especially the PEF one, were more stable during storage than the control.

Roadmap

A possible first approach

Simple and practical. Each step informs the next, and we can stop or change direction at any point.

  1. Understand the product

    Learn about composition, mode of action, application methods, dosage, technical evidence and regulatory status.

  2. Identify priority markets

    Explore sports turf, agricultural crops and professional landscaping. Find the most realistic starting point.

  3. Build local connections

    Identify a small number of growers, clubs, landscaping companies or research partners.

  4. Explore pilot trials

    Assess whether small, well-designed trials under local conditions are feasible.

  5. Assess market potential

    Use trial feedback and commercial information to decide where to focus.
